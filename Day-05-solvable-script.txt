Title: Day 5 — Dominance-solvable games: one cell left means one equilibrium
Voices: NOA = Heart, TOM = Fenrir
Output: Day-05-solvable.mp3

NOA: Let's talk about what it means when the cutting leaves you with a single cell. The course has a name for that kind of game. Dominance-solvable.
TOM: So the grid shrinks all the way down to one cell.
NOA: Down to one cell. And that cell is an equilibrium. But here is the part the exam loves. It is the only equilibrium. Not the only pure one. The only one at all.
TOM: Wait. I keep hearing that every game has a mixed one hiding somewhere.
NOA: Every game has at least one equilibrium, that is Nash's theorem, and here that one is the surviving cell. A mixed strategy is a recipe made out of moves you are willing to play. Think of a cook who can only use what is still in the kitchen. Every move we cut lost no matter what the other side did, so it is gone. There is nothing left to mix with.
TOM: So an empty pantry means no recipe.
NOA: Exactly. Now look at the warning box on the page, the one about the trap. When a question asks how many equilibria, pure or mixed, those two words whisper that a mixed one must be lurking somewhere. They are bait.
TOM: So I check one thing first. Does the cutting end in a single cell? If yes, I answer one, and I stop.
NOA: Answer one, and stop. Even if the words pure or mixed make you nervous.

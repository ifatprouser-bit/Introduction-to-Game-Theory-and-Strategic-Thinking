Title: Day 4 — What the equilibrium test actually asks
Voices: NOA = Heart, TOM = Fenrir
Output: Day-04-what-equilibrium-means.mp3

NOA: Let's start with the definition, and it's shorter than people expect. Each player's choice is a best reply to the other player's choice. That's the whole thing.

TOM: Best reply meaning — given what she did, I could not have done better?

NOA: Exactly that. Not better than her. Better than your own other options, with her choice held still.

TOM: So it's really a test about regret. Nobody looks back and wishes they had moved on their own.

NOA: That's a good way to say it. No private regrets anywhere in the cell. And now notice what the test never asks. It never asks whether the outcome is nice.

TOM: Ah. So a cell could be lovely for both of them and still fail the test?

NOA: Look at the arrows on the page, leaving the friendly-looking corner. Both players want to walk away from it. Lovely, and unstable.

TOM: And I suppose the opposite happens too. Somewhere miserable that neither of them will leave.

NOA: The highlighted cell. Both would rather be in the friendly corner. Neither will move alone, because moving alone makes things worse for whoever moves.

TOM: So good and stable are two different questions, and the exam only ever asks the second one.

NOA: Hold onto that sentence. Mixing up those two questions is the most common way people drop a mark here.

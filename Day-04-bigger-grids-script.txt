Title: Day 4 — Bigger grids and the counting question
Voices: NOA = Heart, TOM = Fenrir
Output: Day-04-bigger-grids.mp3

NOA: A wider grid is not harder. It is only longer.

TOM: Same routine, more of it.

NOA: Same routine, more of it. But there is a new risk on a wide grid, and it isn't cleverness. It's fatigue. The marking takes real time, and the last column is where attention goes.

TOM: And the options on a counting question are just counts, aren't they? So a missed cell doesn't hand me a strange answer.

NOA: It hands you a tidy wrong one. Every count you could possibly produce is offered, including none at all.

TOM: So there's no silly option to warn me. I can't sanity-check my way out.

NOA: You can't. Which gives you one habit worth having. Finish all the marking before you start counting. Never count as you go.

TOM: And then there's the follow-up question you mentioned. The one that comes right after.

NOA: It asks for the highest the row player can earn in an equilibrium. So: find every equilibrium first. Then look only at the row player's number inside those cells. Then take the biggest of those.

TOM: Not the biggest number anywhere on the grid.

NOA: Never that. The biggest number on the grid usually sits somewhere unstable — and it is always, always one of the options.

TOM: Because it's exactly what a tired person would grab.

NOA: Look at the marked grid on the page and find it. It's there.
